Our story

From one oven, one recipe book, and a lot of patience.

Crumb & Craft began as a small neighbourhood oven: bread mixed the night before, shaped by hand at 4 AM, and sold out by breakfast. Today the routine is the same, only the oven is bigger. We use local wheat, cultured butter, Belgian chocolate and seasonal fruit — and we still taste every batch before it reaches the counter.

The story, in three chapters

Warm bread. Patient hands. A life, marked sweetly.

Every loaf and cake at Crumb & Craft follows the same quiet rhythm — early, slow, and made for someone by name, not for a shelf.

Chapter I

Before Sunrise

At 4 a.m. the oven is already warm and the first dough is cool from its overnight rest. Hands dusted with flour shape each loaf slowly, so the crust crackles when it cools and the crumb stays soft inside. The first smell is butter melting into pastry, then cardamom, then warm bread drifting out before the shutters even go up.

This is why the 7:30 a.m. bread tastes different: it has not waited for you. You have arrived just in time for it.

Chapter II

Recipes That Travelled With Family

Our recipes did not start in a factory manual. They travelled in notebooks, in the memory of a grandmother's festival cake, in a father's Sunday bread, in the exact pinch of cardamom that makes a bun taste like home. We kept the soul of those recipes and refined only the technique — slower fermentation, cultured butter, real vanilla — so a childhood taste can survive, beautifully, in a grown-up bakery.

Seasonal fruit, local wheat and Belgian chocolate are chosen the same way: would we serve this at our own table?

Chapter III

The Cake That Marks a Life

A birthday, a first job, a wedding, a baby's first year — a cake is rarely just a cake. It is the moment the room goes quiet, the candles are lit, and everyone leans in. So we bake celebration cakes fresh on the day, finish them by hand, and write the name carefully, as if it were our own family at the table. When the cake is cut, the memory should taste as sweet as it looks.

That is why we ask for 48 hours: not to delay you, but to give a life's moment the time it deserves.

“We do not bake for the counter. We bake for the morning you will remember.”

A note from our head baker

“I still taste every batch before it leaves the kitchen. If a croissant is not flaky enough, or a loaf has not sung when it cools, it does not go out. Warm bread forgives nothing — and that honesty is what I want you to taste.”
