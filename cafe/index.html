Our story

FROM A SMALL ROASTER
TO YOUR CORNER TABLE.

We started with one roaster, two chairs and a stubborn idea: coffee should taste like the place it came from. Today we roast small batches every Tuesday and Friday, grind to order, and serve food made in our own kitchen — no reheated shortcuts, no syrupy shortcuts.

🌱 Chikmagalur beans

Arabica from small farms, roasted light-medium so you can taste chocolate, jaggery and orange — not just bitterness.

🍞 Baked at 5 am

Sourdough, brioche and cakes leave our oven before the town wakes up. When the cake board says sold out, it really is sold out.

🤝 A room for everyone

Students with laptops, families after temple visits, friends catching up, and grandparents reading the paper. Everyone gets the same warm welcome.

The story, in three chapters

ONE CUP, THREE QUIET ACTS.

This is not a cafe that rushes you. The story moves the way a good morning should: from the hills, to the slow brew, to the table where the day actually begins.

I.

Origin of the Bean

It starts high in the Chikmagalur hills, where small farms pick ripe Arabica cherries by hand. The beans arrive in small lots, not in bulk, and we roast them here in light-medium batches every Tuesday and Friday.

When the roast is right, the room fills with a warm, clear aroma: dark chocolate first, then jaggery, then a soft lift of orange. You can smell the place the coffee came from before you taste it.

II.

The Slow Brew

At 5 am the oven is already warm and the first dough is rising. Filter coffee drips slowly, cold brew steeps for 18 hours, and the kaapi is frothed the traditional dabara way until it turns silky and hot.

The first sip should not be hurried. It should be warm on the hands, gentle on the tongue, and steady enough to make the rest of the morning feel possible.

“We do not sell speed here. We sell the ten minutes when the day still belongs to you.”— The Brew & Bean house note
III.

The Table Where Life Happens

On monsoon mornings in Kundapura, rain drums on the terrace roof, steam rises from brass dabaras, and the cafe fills with the town waking up. A student finishes an assignment. Grandparents read the paper. Friends who have not met in months order one more cake and stay for one more story.

That table is the point of everything before it. The bean, the roast and the slow brew only matter when they reach a person, on an ordinary day, and make it feel a little more cared for.

“People do not remember the coffee alone. They remember who they were sitting with when they drank it.”— A regular, Friday open-mic night

A note from the founder

I started this cafe for the mornings I missed most.

I grew up with filter kaapi at home, poured slowly and talked over slowly. When I opened Brew & Bean, I wanted that same feeling in a room anyone could walk into: honest beans, bread baked before dawn, and no pressure to leave. If you stay for a second cup, that is the compliment I care about most.

— Meera, founder and head roaster (demo brand note)
